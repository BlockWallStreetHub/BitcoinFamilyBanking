The idea behind infinite banking

You already finance everything you own. The question is who earns the interest.

Almost every large purchase is financed. If you borrow from a bank, you pay the bank interest. If you pay cash, you give up the interest that money could have earned. Either way, someone else collects. Nelson Nash, who wrote Becoming Your Own Banker, argued that you can capture some of that flow for yourself by building a pool of capital you control and borrowing from it.

How it works with whole life insurance

  1. You pay premiums into a whole life policy built to grow cash value quickly.
  2. The cash value is yours to borrow against. The insurer lends you money using the cash value as collateral, and your policy keeps growing.
  3. You repay the loan with interest. The "honest banker" rule is to repay on a schedule as if a stranger lent it, so the pool refills.
  4. If you never repay, the loan is taken out of the death benefit when you die.

Why people like it

Access to cash without a credit application, a growing asset with contractual guarantees, and a habit of paying yourself back. The deeper point is behavioral: you stop treating borrowing as something only banks do.

The honest limits

Cash value builds slowly in the early years and the policy carries commissions and costs. A policy can lapse if loans plus interest outgrow its cash value, and there are tax traps if it is funded the wrong way. Salespeople often oversell it. Treat infinite banking as a way of behaving, which is owning your capital base and financing yourself, rather than as a product you must buy.

Three ideas to keep

  • Own the capital base. Build an asset you control that others do not.
  • Borrow against it; do not sell it. The asset keeps working while you use the cash.
  • Be the bank. Pay interest back into your own system instead of an outsider's.

Check yourself

Where does the interest go when you borrow from a bank, and where does it go when you pay cash?

To the bank when you borrow. When you pay cash, you lose the interest the cash could have earned, so the cost is hidden.

What does the "honest banker" rule require?

Repaying your own loan on a schedule, with interest, so the pool refills. Without it the system is just a debt you owe yourself.

What really changes

A policy's cash value is a contract. It does not fall when markets fall, and the insurer cannot force a sale because prices dropped. Bitcoin has no such promise. Its price can fall 70% while your loan stays the same size. That one difference is why this course spends so much time on loan size, buffers, and rules.

Bitcoin also has upside a policy does not: an asset that can grow in value, held without an intermediary. Some families will keep both, using a policy as the steady layer and Bitcoin as the growth layer. They do different jobs.

The one-line version

A Bitcoin family bank is a vault you never sell, a loan you can survive, a pool you run honestly, and a legal moat around all of it.

Check yourself

Name the biggest structural difference between a policy loan and a Bitcoin-backed loan.

A Bitcoin-backed loan can be liquidated if the collateral falls too far. A policy loan has no margin call, though a policy can still lapse if its loans outgrow the cash value.

Why does the Bitcoin version need its own inheritance plan?

Bitcoin has no built-in beneficiary. Without keys, instructions, and legal structure, heirs may not be able to reach it.

Layer 1: The vault

The vault is Bitcoin you do not sell. Everything else rests on keeping it safe.

The vault is your collateral and your long-term savings in one. Set a rule early: the core stack is not sold to fund spending. You borrow against it instead. The rule works only if you can always reach the Bitcoin, which comes down to custody.

Test recovery before you borrow

Practice restoring the wallet from backups on a fresh device while the amount is small and there is no loan. A key problem discovered after you owe money is the worst time to learn it.

Borrowing on Aave moves part of the vault

Aave lends against wrapped Bitcoin held in a smart contract, not against coins in cold storage. To borrow, you swap some Bitcoin for a wrapped version and post it there. Decide how much of the vault stays in cold storage as the untouched core and how much goes in as collateral, and write it down.

Vault checklist

  • Decide the core stack: the amount that is never sold for spending.
  • Choose a custody setup and write down why.
  • Store backups in separate places that a single event cannot reach.
  • Restore the wallet from backup on a fresh device and confirm the balance.
  • Write recovery instructions in plain language that a trusted person could follow.
  • Set a recurring deposit, so the vault grows on a schedule.
  • Decide how much stays in cold storage and how much is posted to Aave as collateral.

Check yourself

Why is an exchange account not a vault?

The exchange holds the coins and you hold a claim on the company. If it fails or freezes withdrawals, you may not reach your Bitcoin.

Why restore from backup before taking a loan?

It proves you can recover the keys. A loan raises the stakes, because losing access while in debt can mean losing the collateral and still owing money.

Layer 2: The bank

You borrow stablecoins against the vault. The loan must be small enough to survive a crash.

The key measure is loan-to-value (LTV): what you owe divided by what the vault is worth. The lending platform sets a liquidation LTV. On Aave a smart contract enforces it: if you reach it, a liquidator repays part of your debt and takes some of your Bitcoin, plus a bonus. No one calls you first. Bitcoin can drop 70% or more, and LTV rises as the price falls. A loan that looks tiny in a bull market can be fatal in a crash.

Calculator: how far can it fall?

Aave sets the real liquidation line, and it can change, so use the number shown on Aave today. Aave also shows a health factor: your collateral's liquidation value divided by your debt. Below 1.0, the loan can be liquidated.

Lenders and protocols can fail

Several crypto lenders went bankrupt in 2022, and customers found their collateral caught in the proceedings. Learn whether a lender can reuse your collateral, who holds the keys, and what happens if the company fails. On Aave there is no company to go bankrupt, but the code, the wrapped-Bitcoin issuer, and the price feeds that trigger liquidations can still fail.

Rules that keep the bank alive

  • Cap the loan. Choose the cap by working backwards from a 70% drop, as the calculator shows.
  • Keep a cash buffer. Hold stablecoins you can use to repay part of the loan in a hurry.
  • Set alerts. Get a warning well before the liquidation line.
  • Write down the plan. Decide in advance what you will do at each LTV level, so you do not decide in a panic.

Borrowing is generally not treated as a sale, but tax rules and the treatment of interest depend on your situation. Confirm with a CPA.

Check yourself

You borrow 20% of the vault's value and the price falls 70%. What is your LTV?

About 67%. The debt stays the same while the vault shrinks to 30% of its value: 20 ÷ 30. Interest would push it higher.

Why can a loan be safe at the start and dangerous later?

LTV changes with price. The debt is fixed in dollars while the collateral swings, so a fall raises LTV even though you borrowed nothing more.

Layer 3: The family pool

The borrowed money goes into a pool the family lends from. Interest that family members pay stays in the family.

This is the honest banker loop. Family members, or family businesses, borrow from the pool, sign simple terms, and repay with interest. The pool then pays the interest on the Aave loan and keeps the rest. In a policy, the insurer runs this machinery. Here, the family has to run it.

Where the pool's money comes from

You are not borrowing from an outside person or company. The pool is funded from your own side, and Aave is the machinery that lets you do it without selling Bitcoin:

  • Stablecoins borrowed against the vault. Aave lends them against the Bitcoin you post. You pay Aave's variable rate on every dollar, lent out or not, and the loan can be liquidated.
  • Your own stablecoins. New savings or earnings you put in directly. There is no borrowing cost and no liquidation, so this is the safest source.
  • Income from outside yield farming. Earnings from pools or lending outside the family bank that you route in. Treat it as income that can shrink, not a promise.

One thing to be clear about: the Aave interest does not come back to you. It goes to the people who supplied the stablecoins. Only the interest your family pays the pool stays in your system.

Set the rate with your Aave borrowing cost in mind

The pool owes Aave interest on all the money it borrowed, but it earns interest only on the part it lends out, and some loans go unpaid. The cash you do not lend can earn something too, so there is a box for that. A family rate that looks generous can still leave the pool behind. Try your own numbers below.

Running the pool well

  • Put every loan in writing. Amount, rate, repayment schedule, and what happens if a payment is missed.
  • Start small. Make the first loans small enough that a default is a lesson rather than a crisis.
  • Keep cash back. Cash you do not lend is the buffer that rescues the bank layer in a crash.
  • Treat the pool as separate. Keep its records apart from household spending.
  • Enforce the terms kindly and consistently. Quiet forgiveness teaches the family the rules are optional.
The honest result

With the numbers above, lending at a modest premium to Aave's rate loses a little once bad loans and idle cash are counted. The family bank pays when the borrowed money earns more than it costs, such as funding a family business with real returns. The pool alone rarely does that.

Check yourself

Why does the pool pay interest on all the borrowed money but earn on only part of it?

Aave charges on the full balance. The pool earns only on the portion lent out, and the rest sits in cash as a buffer.

What is the break-even family rate?

Aave's cost per dollar actually lent, less anything the idle cash earns, plus the share of loans that go bad. The calculator shows it for your inputs.

If the money comes from my own Bitcoin, why is there interest at all?

Because the stablecoins come from the people who supplied them to Aave, not from your Bitcoin. Your Bitcoin is only the collateral. Aave pays those suppliers, and you pay Aave. The only interest that stays in the family is what family members pay the pool.

Layer 4: The moat

A strong vault can still be lost to death, divorce, debt, or disagreement. The moat is the legal and human structure around it.

Wealthy families have long built layers of protection around what they own. The same logic applies to Bitcoin, with an extra problem: coins that nobody can reach are gone. These are the four threats and the tools that address them.

ThreatWhat can go wrongTools to discuss with your attorney
DeathHeirs cannot find or move the Bitcoin, or it passes in a messy wayMultisig with heir access, written recovery instructions, a trust, a will, and possibly life insurance for liquidity
DivorceThe vault is split or must be sold to pay a settlementPrenuptial or postnuptial agreements and the way assets are titled
Debt and lawsuitsCreditors reach assets held in your own nameEntity and trust structures, liability insurance, and keeping risky activity separate
DisagreementSiblings or partners fight over loans, spending, or a saleA family constitution, named trustees, and written voting rules
No structure is a guarantee

How well trusts and entities protect assets depends on the type of structure, the state or country, and how you use it. A structure set up wrongly, or after trouble starts, may protect nothing. Hire a specialist who has handled digital assets.

Moat checklist for your attorney meeting

  • List what you own and how each item is titled today.
  • Ask which trust or entity structure fits your goals and your state.
  • Ask how a trust or entity should hold Bitcoin and its keys.
  • Decide who the trustees and backup trustees are.
  • Ask how heirs get access, and how long it takes.
  • Ask whether a life insurance policy belongs beside the Bitcoin.
  • Bring your draft family constitution from the next lesson.

Check yourself

Which of the four threats is a people problem rather than a legal one?

Disagreement. A written constitution, clear roles, and regular meetings settle it better than a legal structure alone.

Why set up protections before trouble starts?

Moving assets into a structure after a lawsuit or claim appears can be undone by a court. Early, ordinary planning is far stronger.

Layer 5: Generations

Systems fail when the next generation inherits money without the knowledge or rules to use it.

You will often hear that most wealthy families lose their wealth by the third generation. The exact figures are debated, but the pattern is common enough to plan for. The usual cause is a lack of preparation and agreement, not a shortage of assets. A written set of family rules is the cure.

How the model works

The model steps forward one month at a time. On day one it borrows the starting share of the vault and lends part of it to the family. Then each month it does this, in order:

  1. Sets the Bitcoin price from the scenario line, plus the noise.
  2. Buys Bitcoin with the monthly deposit and adds it to the vault.
  3. Charges interest on the loan and pays it from the pool's cash. If cash runs short, unpaid interest is added to the loan.
  4. Collects interest from family loans, writes off the share that goes bad, and takes in the monthly repayments.
  5. Lends money out again until the lent-out share is back at the setting.
  6. If "Borrow more as the vault grows" is on, borrows up to the starting percentage again.
  7. Checks LTV. If it is at the rescue trigger and the pool has cash, the pool repays debt, aiming at the borrowing cap.
  8. Checks LTV against the liquidation line. If it is at or past it, Aave sells enough Bitcoin to clear the debt plus the penalty, and the loan resets to zero.

The LTV chart shows the value from before the rescue in step 7, so a rescue appears as a spike and a dot instead of disappearing.

What the model leaves out

It does not include taxes, trading or custody fees, platform-specific rules such as partial liquidations or margin calls with a grace period, or the failure of a lender, protocol, wrapped-Bitcoin issuer, or price feed. It also leaves out yield on the cash you hold back and any outside yield farming income you route into the pool, so it can understate the family bank if you put idle cash to work. The pool cannot recall family loans in an emergency, so only cash counts toward a rescue. Real price drops can happen inside a single month, faster than the monthly steps show. Use it to understand the structure, not as a forecast.

Common questions

Why did my numbers change when I did nothing?

You pressed Reshuffle. It draws new random price noise. The same noise is used for every scenario in the table, so the scenarios stay comparable with each other.

I set "Borrow at the start" to 40% but it borrowed less.

The cap wins. The model borrows the lower of the starting share and "Never borrow past," so raise both.

The banner says held. Is my plan safe?

No. It means this one path, with this noise, did not trigger a liquidation. Run every scenario in the table, reshuffle a few times, and look at the peak LTV, not just the outcome. A peak near the liquidation line is a near miss.

Why does the rescue stop helping in a long crash?

The pool's cash is finite. Each rescue spends cash on debt, and cash refills only from family repayments and interest. A second drop after a rescue meets a thinner buffer.

What should I set the liquidation line to?

Use the number Aave or your platform publishes. If you are unsure, test a lower line such as 70%, which is harsher than the 80% default.

Your rollout plan

Build from the inside out, and add risk only after the layer beneath is proven.

  1. Vault and custody. Choose your setup, test recovery, and begin recurring deposits.
  2. Constitution and legal wrapper. Draft the family rules and meet your attorney and CPA.
  3. A conservative credit line. Take a small loan, run a full repay cycle, and watch your alerts.
  4. Open the pool. Make first loans small, in writing, and on schedule.
  5. Add heirs and education. Bring heirs into the key setup and start the teaching track.

Rollout checklist

  • Vault set up and recovery tested.
  • Family constitution drafted and reviewed with family.
  • Attorney and CPA consulted, with their advice in writing.
  • First loan taken at a low LTV with alerts running.
  • First family loan made, documented, and repaid.
  • Heirs added and recovery practiced.
Stop rules

Do not borrow if you cannot explain your liquidation price from memory, if your recovery test has not passed, or if you would be forced to sell at a bad price to cover living costs. Repay or reduce the loan first.

Main risks to review

  • Liquidation in a sharp drop
  • Protocol, lender, or wrapped-Bitcoin failure
  • Lost or unreachable keys
  • Changes in tax or regulation
  • Borrowing for things that do not earn a return
